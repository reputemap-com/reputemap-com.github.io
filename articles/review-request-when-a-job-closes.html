Send a Google review request automatically when a job closes

Most home-service and clinic owners agree that the best moment to ask for a Google review is right after the job: the plumber just left, the invoice is paid, the visit is fresh. The worst way to do it is by hand, because by Friday nobody remembers who to ask.

The fix is boring and reliable: your job tool fires a webhook when a job closes, and something sends the review request. Here’s the pattern we built at ReputeMap, and the details that decide whether it works in real life.

The shape of it

Every workspace gets a private trigger URL, and your job tool posts the customer to it (JSON or form fields both work):

POST https://api.reputemap.com/api/hooks/jobs/rmj_…
{"first_name":"Ava","email":"ava@example.com","job_id":"J-1042","location":"Austin","staff":"Jesse Ramirez"}

ReputeMap then sends the same review request a person would send from the app: by email, or by text from the business’s own Quo (OpenPhone) or Twilio number when there’s only a phone.

Wiring it up

  • Zapier: trigger = your tool’s “job completed” or “invoice paid”, action = Webhooks by Zapier → POST to the trigger URL (needs a paid Zapier plan).
  • Make: your tool’s module → HTTP → Make a request, method POST, body type JSON.
  • n8n: your trigger node → HTTP Request node, POST, JSON body.
  • Housecall Pro: its Webhooks app (MAX plan) can post job.completed straight to a location’s own trigger URL.
  • Jobber: through Zapier’s “Job Closed” trigger.

Five details that matter more than the webhook

1. Forgiving field names. Every tool names things differently: “First Name”, customer_email, customer[email], a nested customer object. We normalise case, spaces, dashes and common prefixes, and look inside wrappers like data or payload. Otherwise every new tool turns into a support ticket.

2. Never ask twice. Webhooks get retried and Zaps get duplicated. The job, invoice or appointment id is the de-duplication key: the same job never sends twice. On top of that, anyone asked in the last 30 days, by any location or campaign, is skipped.

3. Wait a little. Requests go out 2 hours after the event by default. “Job closed” often fires while the technician is still in the driveway.

4. Respect the quiet. Quiet hours hold requests at night and on weekends, and opt-outs from any channel are honoured everywhere.

5. Show what happened. A “Send a test event” button runs the whole pipeline without sending anything, and a log of the last 50 events says what happened in plain words: queued, sent, skipped (duplicate job, asked recently, opted out) or rejected (no email or phone, unknown location). Silent failures are the number-one complaint about review-request tools, and a log is the cheapest cure.

One rule we won’t break

Every customer gets the same request. There’s no “only ask the happy ones” filter and no way to cancel a queued request because the job went badly. Google’s review policies and the FTC’s 2024 rule on consumer reviews both forbid review gating, and it backfires anyway.
